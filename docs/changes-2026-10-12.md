## 3. Calibration fix (c35349b)

The per-run timing that picks the pruning mode rated 16-4's pdbparent at 1.0-1.1x vs off,
while full runs show 1.45-1.5x (16-4 at 13 moves: 1016-1034 s off vs 682-708 s pdbparent). So
16-4's first 14-move attempt ran with no bound at all. Two causes, found one after the other:

1. **Modes timed side by side.** Seeds of every mode ran at once on all 16 threads and disturbed
   each other's cache and memory traffic (pdbleaf alone costs 3-4x per seed). Fix: each block of
   512 seeds runs one mode on every thread; blocks rotate through the modes; modes are compared
   round by round (log time ratios); a mode clearly slower (> 5% and > 3 standard errors) is
   dropped after 4 rounds; timing continues until the rest are told apart (2 SE) or known to ~1%,
   within 3% of the pass's estimated search (20 s minimum, `--bound-calibrate-seconds` maximum,
   default 300 s). Result on 16-4 at 13 moves: right mode, but still 1.11x.
2. **Only the first ~1% of the seed list was ever timed.** The list is sorted (similar boards
   side by side) and pruning strength differs a lot between its regions. Fix: seeds are visited
   in 4096-seed chunks with a coprime golden-ratio stride, so any stretch of the visiting order
   is spread over the list. Each pass now checks that it streamed every seed exactly once.
   Result: **1.61x** (+-13%) vs off, full runs 1.45-1.5x; search time unchanged (707 s).

Exact: 9-3 (13) and 10-3 (1648) at 13 moves, identical sets.

## 4. The rotation lane bound (ddcd26b)

### The idea

A row move only rotates its row, so the row's cyclic arrangement is unchanged; a column move
changes exactly one cell of every row. For each row take the fewest cells that differ from the
target row over the row's 6 rotations (its rotation distance). Then:
- column moves needed >= the largest row rotation distance (row moves never change it; each
  column move lowers each row's by at most 1),
- row moves needed >= the largest column rotation distance (mirror image),
- h = the sum; it is never below colour mix (a row's rotation distance >= its multiset
  mismatch) and changes by at most 1 per move, so the existing pruning (leaf skips, parent
  prune at every level) works unchanged.

Example: target row `R R G G B B`, row `R G B R G B`: colour mix 0 (same colours), rotation
distance 4 (every rotation has 4 cells wrong).

On 2x2 boards a move on the piece's lanes replaces two cells per row, so the bound is halved and
prunes nothing (section 7).

### Fast form

Same lookup as colour mix: per lane, two 9-bit triples -> `first[L][a] + second[L][b]` -> a
one-hot distance byte; 12 lanes, 11 ORs, 2 bit scans. Only the tables differ: each cell becomes
a symbol (one of the target lane's m colours, or "other"), a triple is 3 symbols in base S =
m + 1, and the lane table holds all S^6 distances (0.2-1.2 MB per puzzle). Cell positions matter
now: in a row the low triple is cells 5, 4, 3; in a column the `pext` of the top half is rows
2, 1, 0. `referenceBound(state, target, kind)` is the plain definition the audit compares
against. `--bound-kind rotation|mix` (default rotation).

## 5. Other pruning analyses (not built)

- **Goal symmetry** (spatial D4 x torus translations x colour permutations fixing the goal):
  normal goals 12-1: 144, 20-2: 48, 14-4 and 18-3: 24, 15-1: 12, 14-3 and 16-2: 8; 2x2 goals 1-2.
  No puzzle has a symmetry fixing both start and goal. It could shrink goal tables |G|-fold, at the
  cost of canonicalizing every lookup; the rotation bound makes it unnecessary for now.
- **One-cell / rare colours** (19-5 one red, 20-5 one yellow, 19-4 one cyan; 14-4, 14-5, 20-4,
  16-1 have 2-cell colours): a single tile alone bounds at most 2 moves, and the rotation bound
  already counts a misplaced rare tile as a wrong cell. A pattern table that tracks a rare colour
  as its own symbol (e.g. 1 exact cell + an 8-cell class: 846 M entries, 211 MB) would know more,
  but only 14-5 and 20-4 are both rare-coloured and weakly pruned; at most ~1.5-2x there.
- **2x2 boards cannot be helped by any lane bound**: see section 7.
- **Next candidates**: move-type-split pattern tables (count column moves with row moves free
  and vice versa, then add the best column bound to the best row bound); the same split on normal
  boards (~1.2-2x on weak 15-move puzzles). Neither measured yet.

## 7. Why lane bounds cannot prune 2x2 boards

On a 2x2 board the moves on the piece's two lanes shift two lanes at once, replacing two
neighbouring cells of every row (or column). The sharpest per-lane count is then: over the lane's
rotations, the fewest single cells or neighbouring pairs that cover its wrong cells. A lane never
needs more than 3 of those (a fully wrong lane is 3 pairs; three isolated wrong cells are 3
singles), so worst row + worst column <= 6. 2x2 searches only prune above the goal side's depth
(7): no bound of this kind can ever cut anything there. A bound for 2x2 boards has to look at
several lanes at once (pattern tables) or the gain has to come from elsewhere (fewer passes,
the GPU). This is why the roadmap puts the compact index first.

## 8. Decisions and answers

- **Sharding for restart safety: rejected** (13-33% slower: every shard redoes the setup). An
  in-run checkpoint (no cost without a restart, ~10-15 min per restart) is deferred until runs
  take days. Restarts happen between jobs.
- **6-4 at 16 moves**: ~150-160 h on this CPU (2x2: the rotation bound does not help), ~70-80 h
  with the compact index, ~15-30 h once ported to the GPU.
- **6-4 at 14 moves now**: ~1 h estimated (other 2x2 puzzles: 48-68 min); 2 h 35 min measured on
  2026-10-06; a 14-move run once took "at least 2 weeks" on another computer (puzzle not
  recorded; no trace in the repo).

## 9. Lessons

- **Look for what a move cannot change.** Both lane bounds came from move invariants (row
  contents, then row order up to rotation); the second is far stronger deep in the search.
- **Timing samples must be representative.** A sorted seed list makes "the first 1%" a biased
  sample; interleaving modes makes them disturb each other.
- **Prototype bounds in Python first**: replay known solutions for admissibility and sample random
  walks for skip rates; the random-walk numbers matched the solver's audits within ~2 points.
- **Test builds go to a separate output folder** (`-DMB_OUTPUT_DIR`): the scheduler copies
  `build/experimentDiskDepth6.exe` at every job start, so `build/` only gets verified code, and is
  rebuilt with the queue paused.
- **`git mv` stages its rename immediately**: an unrelated commit picked up two staged renames
  (fixed before pushing with `git reset --soft HEAD~1`). Check `git diff --cached` before every
  commit when moves are pending.
- **Bash heredocs still mangle `\n`** in Python patch code: write patch scripts with the Write tool.
- Baseline-build every target before a refactor, so pre-existing failures are not mistaken for new
  ones (7 of 19 registered targets did not build before anything was moved).
