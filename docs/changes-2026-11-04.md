## 4. Work units by goal partition group: `--d7-groups G`

A plain seed shard (`--shard k/N`) of a depth-7 run still makes every pass, so each shard
rebuilds every pass's index. That costs ~1 min per pass on the 5060 Ti, but 7-9 min on one
Polaris GPU (26-30 regenerations of the depth-7 keys per pass, one per sort batch, on the first
device), so 5-6 index builds per shard.

`--d7-groups G` with `--shard k/N` (N a multiple of G, table depth 7) makes unit k search goal
partition group k / (N/G) (256/G of the key partitions; with G = the run's pass count, one
pass) against seed shard k % (N/G). Units 0..N-1 cover every (partition, seed) pair once, so
their middles together are exactly the full run's. The middles file says `d7groups G`, and
`--recover-from` checks that every unit is there exactly once with the same G (files without
the line are G = 1, as before). Each unit builds one index. Units are also the restart points
and can run on different machines.

Scheduler: `mbq add P --moves 17 --gpu 5060 --threads 6 --shards N --d7-groups G [--remote ...]`
(and `mbq export ... --d7-groups G`). `--shards` jobs now get `--gpu` too: the `--shards`
branch of `mbq add` used to drop it, so GPU shard jobs would have run on the CPU.

Check (friend's PC): 17-2 at 14 as `<2,5,7>` in 5 units (`--shard k/5 --d7-groups 5`, one pass
each, 8 min each: 7.3 min index, 18 s stream), then the merge against the 1053 known solutions:
16 + 14 + 19 + 17 + 15 middles, 81 unique, **EXACT MATCH**, recovery 0.9 s, **IDENTICAL SET**
(1053). No submission over 500 ms.

## 5. Vulkan: the 2-second job limit

The first depth-7 index build over Vulkan (17-2 above, on the friend's PC) lost the device
(`VkResult -4`; the kernel: `ring comp_1.2.0 timeout, but soft recovered`), at different
points in different runs. Linux amdgpu (kernel 7.0) ends a compute job after about 2 s. A new
report (`mbvk: ... one submission took N ms (...)`, over 1 s, `MBVK_SLOW_MS`) caught it: one
radix pass over 348 M keys, histogram, scan and scatter in one submission, took 2029 ms. Every
other submission stayed under 500 ms. The normal-board runs had sorted similar batches without
trouble, just under the limit.

Fix (`mbvk.cpp`, `keys.comp`): histogram, scan and scatter are separate submissions; the
histogram and the scatter run in chunks of tiles (`u1` = the chunk's first tile), and the Bloom
inserts in chunks of keys, each chunk size halved above 250 ms and doubled below 60 ms, per
device at run time. `test_vk_stream vectors.bin`: PASS. The CUDA module is unchanged.

## 8. Where a 2x2 pass's time goes

Every pass re-streams the whole left side, which is why the notes have assumed that the pass
count sets the cost (roadmap item 1: a smaller index, fewer passes). The pass times above say
otherwise. A run's last, smaller group of partitions is much faster although it streams exactly
as many states:

| Run | Full pass (partitions) | Last pass (partitions) |
|---|---|---|
| 15-4 | ~5 950 s (48) | 2 706 s (16) |
| 19-4 | ~6 000 s (47) | 3 263 s (21) |
| 16-1 | ~6 415 s (53) | 5 314 s (44) |
| 15-3 | ~5 720 s (45) | 3 946 s (31) |
| 6-4 | ~4 000 s (43) | 3 813 s (41) |

Only the leaves whose key falls in the pass's partitions take a Bloom test, so the test count is
proportional to the partitions. A straight line through 15-4's and 19-4's passes gives ~100 s
per partition plus ~1 100 s per pass; 15-3's and 16-1's fit a smaller fixed part. This first
reading said the GPU's Bloom tests dominate and that halving the passes would save ~10%.
Section 11 measured it properly: the fixed part is ~1 260 s per pass, and the per-partition
part was the CPU's check of the Bloom survivors (about as fast as the GPU's tests at 10 bits),
so fewer passes through fewer Bloom bits is much slower, not 10% faster. Section 12 sped up the
CPU check instead.

## 11. A pass's cost, measured

Single-pass runs on the same 1/64 of 19-4's seeds (`--shard 0/64`, 8.1e11 left states,
`<5,4,7>`, `--d7-partitions N`, the 5060 Ti, the scheduler's exe), stream + probe time:

| Partitions in the pass | 4 | 24 | 47 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stream + probe | 25.3 s | 53.0 s | 95.1 s |

At 4 partitions the GPU is the limit: ~19.7 s of it is fixed (generating and hashing every
leaf), so **~1 260 s per full pass**. The per-partition part grows with the size of the pass's
Bloom blocks (~1.4 s per partition up to 24, ~1.8 s from 24 to 47), a hint of address
translation or cache effects on the GPU at 11 GB of blocks.

The number of passes is set by the GPU's VRAM, not the RAM: at 10 Bloom bits per goal state,
47 partitions fill the 10.9 GB left for Bloom blocks (6 passes for 19-4), while the 54 GB RAM
budget would allow ~88 per pass. Fewer bits per state means fewer passes but more false
passes. A new `--bloom-bits B` (default 10, no-table modes; same block count as before at 10)
tests it, again on the 1/64 slice, each case the pass size of a full run:

| Bits | Partitions (passes in a full run) | Bloom pass | Stream + probe | Candidates | Verify |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 47 (6) | 1.155% | 91.6 s, 90.4 s | 1.10 M | 6.95 s |
| 9 | 52 (5) | 1.849% | 162.6 s | 1.94 M | 7.42 s |
| 7.3 | 64 (4) | 4.437% | 473.6 s | 5.74 M | 10.53 s |

Fewer bits is far slower. In all three cases the CPU checked the survivors at the same ~19-20 M
per second (6 threads), and at 10 bits that already matches the GPU: the CPU, not the GPU, sets
the pace as soon as the false passes rise. The candidates (false fingerprint matches, held in
RAM until the final check) also grow with them: 317 M in 19-4's whole run at 10 bits would be
~1.4 G at 7 bits, ~34 GB. Fewer bits is the wrong way; more bits (and more passes) is faster,
section 14.

## 12. The survivor check without its second Bloom test

The GPU tests every in-range leaf against the pass's Bloom blocks and hands the survivors to CPU
threads, which ran the full index probe on them: the Bloom test again (always passing: same
blocks, same hash), then the bucket and the fingerprints. The first stage was a wasted random
read into 11 GB per survivor. `FingerprintRange::probePassed` (`fingerprint_index.hpp`) skips it
for the GPU's survivors (`gpuProbePass`, `gpu_stream.hpp`; CUDA and Vulkan alike); the CPU-only
paths keep `probeGroup`.

A/B on the same 1/64 slice at 10 bits, 47 partitions, builds alternated:

| Build | Stream + probe | GPU | CPU finished after the GPU | Candidates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| before | 95.5 s, 98.6 s | 90.2 s, 93.3 s | 3.2 s, 3.1 s | 1 095 635 |
| after | 79.1 s, 79.7 s | 76.9 s, 77.5 s | 0.07 s, 0.09 s | 1 095 635 |

**~18% faster per full pass**, the same candidates and Bloom pass share, and the GPU is the limit
again (the CPU now finishes with it). A pass's last, smaller group was already GPU-bound, so a
whole 6-pass run gains a little less (~15%): 19-4's 9 h 36 min would be ~8 h 10 min, a 17-move
2x2 run ~10 days instead of ~12. With the faster check, 9 bits is still slower (52 partitions:
122.0 s against 79.4 s for 47 at 10 bits).

Exactness (the new build, `--solutions`): 17-2 at 14 (2x2, `<4,3,7>`, 5 passes): 81 middles,
**EXACT MATCH**, 1053 solutions, **IDENTICAL SET**, 330 s (5 min 50 s on 2026-11-02); 16-2 at 15
(normal board, `<5,4,6>`, one pass): 15 middles, **EXACT MATCH**, 366 solutions, **IDENTICAL SET**.

## 13. A move-type-split bound for 2x2 boards (research, negative)

Every solution uses some column moves and some row moves, so

    length >= (fewest column moves with row moves free) + (fewest row moves with column moves free).

The rotation lane bound is this with each half estimated one lane at a time, at most 3 on 2x2
boards. `experimentFatSplitBound` computes each half exactly on an abstract board (the cells of a
colour class plus the piece position, as in `pattern_db.hpp`) by a 0-1 BFS (the free move type
costs nothing), for every class of 1-3 colours up to K cells, takes the max of the tables and the
rotation bound per half, and adds the halves. It checks the result on the puzzle's known
solutions and samples left states of the search.

19-4 (`<5,4,7>`, 40 known solutions, 680 path states):

| Classes | Tables | Highest value of a half | At the start (rotation, split) | Last level, h > 7 | One level up, h > 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| up to 6 cells | 9 x 2 | 4 | 5, 6 | 0.0003% | 0% |
| up to 7 cells | 12 x 2 | 4 | 5, 6 | 0.0086% | 0% |

Admissible (0 violations), and stronger than the rotation bound (mostly 6 where the rotation
bound gives 4-5), but with the other move type free any class of this size is fixed in at most 4
moves, so the sum never passes 8, and pruning needs more than 7 at the last level and more than
8 above it. It would skip under 0.01% of the stream.

The wider reason: with ~33 moves per state, a table of N entries can tell distances apart up to
about log33(N). Tables that fit in RAM (~1e10 entries) reach ~6-7; the all-moves pattern
databases already measured ~0% on 2x2 boards (`pattern_db.hpp` header). Proving a state 9 or more
moves away needs over 33^8 = 1.4e12 entries, and splitting by move type caps each half at 4. So
no pruning bound for 2x2 boards is in reach this way; their time has to come from the search
itself (section 12, more GPUs). Ideas not tried: memory-light search schemes from
cryptanalysis ("dissection", Dinur, Dunkelman, Keller and Shamir, 2012), which attack the memory
limit that keeps the goal side at depth 7; whether they fit row and column shifts is open.

## 14. More Bloom bits: faster, with more passes

Section 11 tried fewer bits per goal state; one of the brainstorm agents (`docs/ideas-2026-11-04.md`)
fitted the same numbers and predicted the opposite direction would win: with more bits each pass
covers fewer partitions (more passes, each paying the ~20 s per slice of generation), but the
false passes, which the survivor path pays for, drop much faster. Measured with the section-12
build on the same 1/64 slice of 19-4 (each case one pass at the size a full run would use; the
run column = passes x the per-pass time, with the partitions spread evenly):

| Bits | Partitions per pass (passes) | One pass | Run, even passes | Bloom pass | Candidates |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 47 (6) | 83.5 s | ~465 s | 1.155% | 1.10 M |
| 12 | 39 (7) | 52.7 s | ~355 s | 0.493% | 0.39 M |
| **14** | **33 (8)** | **42.6 s** | **~335 s** | 0.232% | 0.16 M |
| 16 | 29 (9) | 38.7 s | ~345 s | 0.119% | 0.07 M |

**14 bits: ~28% less time per run than 10** (335 against 465 slice-seconds), and 7x fewer false
candidates to hold in RAM (which matters for 17-move runs). 12 and 16 bits are close behind, so
the optimum is broad. With section 12, a 19-4-sized run would go from ~9 h 36 min to roughly
6 h, and a 17-move 2x2 run from ~12 days to roughly 7. The default stays 10 for now: by the
project's rule the choice should be made per device at run start (a short timed sample at 2-3
settings, cached), not hardcoded from one benchmark; until then pass `--bloom-bits 14`.

## 15. The free-block join (`--block`): one move fewer on the left

Idea 1 of `docs/ideas-2026-11-04.md` (three brainstorm agents found it independently). A row move
only rotates its row, so the **row class** of a board (each row turned to its smallest rotation;
on 2x2 boards the piece's row pair turned so the piece is at column 0, its row kept) is unchanged
by row moves, and two boards share it exactly when one block of row moves on distinct lanes turns
one into the other. Column classes likewise. In a shortest solution of length L the state Y after
L - 8 moves and the state W after L - 7 are one move apart, so they share a row class or a column
class, and W is exactly 7 moves from the goal. So the left side can stop at depth L - 8: each
leaf Y is looked up by its two classes in an index of the depth-7 goal states' classes, and a
goal state W of the same class exactly one lane from Y is a middle, the same middle a plain
`<S,T2+1,7>` run finds. Recovery and `--solutions` are unchanged. (The general form joins across
a whole block of same-direction moves; a block of both directions cannot be absorbed, since no
key is invariant under both.)

### Prototype (`experimentBlockJoin`, CPU, exact distances from both ends)

Every start-side state within A moves looked up by class in the goal states within B, matches
with start distance + lanes that differ + goal distance = A + B + 1 expanded into every full
solution (all shortest prefixes and suffixes, all block orders and periodic-row amounts), each
replayed, then compared with `build/levels_final`:

| Puzzle | Board | Moves (A + B + 1) | Solutions | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8-2 | 2x2 | 8 (4 + 3 + 1) | 92 | IDENTICAL SET |
| 4-2 | 2x2 | 9 (4 + 4 + 1) | 522 | IDENTICAL SET |
| 11-4 | normal | 9 (4 + 4 + 1) | 12 | IDENTICAL SET |
| 13-4 | 2x2 | 10 (5 + 4 + 1) | 10 | IDENTICAL SET |
| 6-5 | 2x2 | 10 (5 + 4 + 1) | 8 | IDENTICAL SET |
| 19-1 | normal | 10 (5 + 4 + 1) | 1329 | IDENTICAL SET |
| 13-3 | normal | 11 (5 + 5 + 1) | 10433 | IDENTICAL SET (626 useful of 1.26 M class matches) |

Goal states per class: 13-3's 58.6 M goal states fall into 13.1 M row classes (and as many
column classes), so an index of classes is smaller than one of states.

### In the solver

- `src/solver/ssd/class_key.hpp`: the class code and key (plain C++ shared with the CUDA module,
  so both compute identical keys), the lane count between two boards of one class.
- GPU module: `mbgpu_set_key_mode`. Mode 1: every leaf gives its two class keys. Mode 2 (what
  `--block` uses): the same set of keys without repeats. A leaf reached by a row move has its
  parent's row class, so each leaf gives only its other class, and each parent gives its two
  classes once (for its row-move and its column-move leaves); a left-side survivor that stands
  for a parent carries `class_key::PARENT_FLAG` (b2 bit 63) and must be two lanes from W instead
  of one. The minimum rotation of a lane comes from a 1 MB table on the GPU. Every leaf mode uses
  the mode: counts, the index build, the Bloom test, the candidate check.
- `--block` (2x2 boards, `--table-depth 7`, `--gpu`, CUDA): `<S,T2,7> + 1` = S + T2 + 8 moves;
  the confirmation regenerates the goal side in mode 1 (the real goal states W) and keeps W when
  it is one lane (two for a parent) from the candidate. Middles files say `block 1`.
- Mode 1 first made the index build 9.6x slower (both classes of every leaf, the rotation loop:
  1 373 s per pass); mode 2 and the table brought it to ~140 s, close to the state keys'.

## 16. `--block` on normal boards, and the direction chosen on the GPU (`--direction`)

**Normal boards.** The normal-board GPU kernel emits each parent's two classes once (key mode 2),
the depth-6 goal side (`no_table_d6.hpp`) is keyed by class, and the bound's cut-off is one higher
(a leaf is still one move from the goal side). The GPU bound audit runs on state keys (the key mode
is switched to 0 for it and back). Recovery is unchanged up to left depth 11; left depth 12

**Direction.** `--direction auto|forward|reverse` (`src/solver/ssd/direction.hpp`). Reverse streams
from the goal and builds the goal side around the start (the old `--reverse` turned the bound,
the GPU and the no-table modes off; those guards are gone). auto, with `--gpu` on normal boards:
before anything is built, a strided sample of each direction's seeds is streamed on the GPU with
the run's bound and depth (grown 4x from 256 seeds until it takes a few seconds), the expanded
nodes are scaled by the seed count, and the cheaper direction is kept. `--recover-from` follows
the middles files' `reverse` line. 2x2 boards (no bound): forward.
