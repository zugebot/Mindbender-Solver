    Game puzzles

    The game's 100 Mind Benders, with the fewest moves each one takes.

    How it works

    Paint the goal, drag the start's Chuzzles to where they begin, then Solve (or open one of the game's puzzles). You get a shortest solution of up to 12 moves (or every one: see Settings), and proof that none is shorter. Replay it: the wand shows each drag, or make the next move yourself by dragging its row or column. It runs on your device in WebAssembly; nothing is uploaded.

    All 100 of the game's puzzles are solved: View shows every shortest solution the desktop solver found (up to 18 moves), with the same replay.

    Notation

    R14
    row 1 shifted right 4
    C32
    column 3 shifted down 2
    R453
    the 2x2 Chuzzle's rows 4 and 5, right 3
    C231
    the 2x2 Chuzzle's columns 2 and 3, down 1

    Rows count from the top, columns from the left. Shifts of 4 or 5 are easier dragged the other way (right 5 = left 1), so the replay's wand drags the short way.

    Editing

    Paint the goal first: pick a colour and click or drag. The 2x2 button at the end of the palette places the big Chuzzle: pick it, then tap where its top-left should go.

    The start begins as a copy of the goal. Drag each of its Chuzzles to where it begins: it swaps places with the one there, so the start always holds the goal's Chuzzles (repainting the goal repaints them on the start too). Tapping two swaps them as well, and the 2x2 is dragged the same way. Mix scrambles the start for you; Reset copies the goal again.

    Picture fills both boards from a screenshot of the game: Chuzzle, Chuzzle 2 or the phone game, the board with its goal beside it. Pick the file, drop it on the page or paste it. The picture is read on your device and goes nowhere. Check the boards against the game before solving.

    Right-click a Chuzzle to pick its colour. Keys 1-8 choose a colour, 9 the 2x2, Ctrl+Z undoes.

    The search

    The solver builds every board within 4 moves of the start and of the goal, and joins the two in the middle. The work is split over several threads, and a lane bound cuts away boards that can't reach the goal in time. It checks every move sequence, so "none shorter" is exact; it stops at the first solution of the shortest length unless Settings asks for all of them, which takes longer. Up to 10 moves takes seconds, 11 moves seconds to a minute, 12 moves from seconds to many minutes: the lane bound cuts away most of the work on boards with several colours, little on boards with two or three colours spread evenly, and nothing with the 2x2 Chuzzle. It stops at 12 moves; longer custom puzzles need the desktop solver.

    Settings

    Stops at the first shortest solution it finds. Every shorter length is still searched in full, so it is still the fewest moves, only much sooner.

    Finds and counts every shortest solution. The last length is searched to the end as well, so a long search can take several times longer.
