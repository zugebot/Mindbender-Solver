    How it works

    Paint both boards, or pick one of the game's 100 puzzles at the top, then Solve. You get every shortest solution of up to 10 moves, and proof that none is shorter. Replay any of them: the wand shows each drag. It runs on your device in WebAssembly; nothing is uploaded.

    Notation

    R14
    row 1 shifted right 4
    C32
    column 3 shifted down 2
    R453
    the 2x2 Chuzzle's rows 4 and 5, right 3
    C231
    the 2x2 Chuzzle's columns 2 and 3, down 1

    Rows count from the top, columns from the left. Shifts of 4 or 5 are easier dragged the other way (right 5 = left 1), so the replay's wand drags the short way.

    Editing

    Pick a colour and click or drag to paint; right-click a cell to pick its colour. The 2x2 button at the end of the palette places the big Chuzzle: pick it, then tap where its top-left should go. Keys 1-8 choose a colour, 9 the 2x2, Ctrl+Z undoes.

    The search

    The solver builds every board within 4 moves of the start and of the goal, and joins the two in the middle. The work is split over several threads, and a lane bound cuts away boards that can't reach the goal in time. It checks every move sequence, so "none shorter" is exact. Up to 8 moves takes about a second, 10 moves seconds to a minute or two. It stops at 10 moves; longer puzzles need the desktop solver.
